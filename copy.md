## SECTION 5 — THE 4Cs (graph, `#how`)

**Label:** HOW IT WORKS

**Headline:**
Curiosity becomes learning [BR]
when it has a path.

### 01 · Curiosity
**Question:** What keeps pulling you back?
**Body:** Students start with something they already care about. The work is not to make that interest more academic, but to find the real question sitting inside it.
**Example prompts:**
- ↓ What do you keep watching, searching, collecting, or arguing about?
- ↓ What part of it do you still not understand?
- ↓ Why does this keep bothering or fascinating you?

### 02 · Clarity
**Question:** What are you really trying to understand?
**Body:** Students sharpen the question before chasing answers. They learn that a broad topic is not yet an inquiry.
**Transformations:**
- "Football" becomes → *Why do some goalkeepers dive before the penalty is kicked?*
- "K-pop" becomes → *How do fan communities make a comeback feel bigger than the song itself?*
- "Gaming" becomes → *Why do some games make losing feel addictive?*

### 03 · Creation
**Question:** What can you build from what you found?
**Body:** Students draw on AI, primary sources, articles, videos, and worked examples to investigate, then turn what they find into something concrete — a theory, a map, a comic, a guide, a prototype, a visual explanation, a playlist with an argument behind it.
**Closer (italic, green):** The format follows the pursuit.

### 04 · Communication
**Question:** Can someone else understand it?
**Body:** Students explain their work to someone who knows little about the topic. Vague thinking gets exposed in that conversation, and so does the moment a student realises they actually understand what they made.
**A good C-Level ends with a student who can say:**
- *This was my question.*
- *This is what I found.*
- *This is what changed my mind.*
- *This is what I made.*
- *This is what I still want to know.*

## SECTION 6 — RESPONSIBLE AI (green)

**Label:** AI USE, MADE VISIBLE

**Headline:**
AI is part of the pursuit. [BR]
It does not get to do the pursuing.

**Body:**
Students use AI to explore, compare, challenge, and organise their thinking. Answers from a model are treated as material to inspect, rather than conclusions to repeat.

Across a C-Level, students practise:

- ↓ asking stronger follow-up questions
- ↓ comparing AI answers with other sources
- ↓ spotting assumptions
- ↓ checking what might be missing
- ↓ separating evidence from confidence
- ↓ explaining the final work in their own words

**Closer (amber italic):**
AI use is not hidden in C-Levels. What we make visible is the thinking around it — the questions asked, the sources checked, the parts the student worked out for themselves.
