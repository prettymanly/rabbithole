THE AI MANDATE IS HERE

Schools have access to AI.
The harder question is what students do with it.

AI literacy is now part of school life. Students are being introduced to tools, prompts, bias, chatbots, and responsible use, and that work matters.

What a tool cannot do is decide which questions are worth asking, notice when an answer is too easy, or check whether the student in front of it actually understands what just came back.

That part still belongs to the student.

C-Levels treats curiosity as something a student can practise, rather than perform.

WHAT CAN GET MISSED

Students can generate answers
they do not understand.

A student types a question, AI responds, and the work that comes back already looks polished.

  • But did they frame the question well?
  • Did they check the source?
  • Did they notice what was missing?
  • Can they explain the answer without hiding behind the screen?

C-Levels is built for that gap. It works on the habits AI cannot do for a student: framing a question with care, testing the answer it gives back, noticing what is missing, and being able to explain the work in their own words afterwards.

THE FORMAT

A C-Level is a
learning pursuit.

A C-Level is a guided inquiry where a student follows one curiosity deeply and returns with something teachable.

It is not a worksheet, a prompt challenge, or a slide deck rushed in an afternoon.

It is a pursuit with a clear outcome.

A student begins with something they keep coming back to — a game mechanic, a football tactic, a manga world, a song lyric they cannot place, a family language they half-understand, a question that has been living rent-free in their head.

From there, they learn how to follow it properly: sharpening the question, investigating with AI and primary sources, building something from what holds up, and teaching it back to a person who has not been in the room.

By the end, the curiosity has become visible.

HOW IT WORKS

Curiosity becomes learning
when it has a path.

01 · Curiosity

What keeps pulling you back?

Students start with something they already care about. The work is not to make that interest more academic, but to find the real question sitting inside it.

Example prompts

  • What do you keep watching, searching, collecting, or arguing about?
  • What part of it do you still not understand?
  • Why does this keep bothering or fascinating you?

02 · Clarity

What are you really trying to understand?

Students sharpen the question before chasing answers. They learn that a broad topic is not yet an inquiry.

"Football" becomes

Why do some goalkeepers dive before the penalty is kicked?

"K-pop" becomes

How do fan communities make a comeback feel bigger than the song itself?

"Gaming" becomes

Why do some games make losing feel addictive?

03 · Creation

What can you build from what you found?

Students draw on AI, primary sources, articles, videos, and worked examples to investigate, then turn what they find into something concrete — a theory, a map, a comic, a guide, a prototype, a visual explanation, a playlist with an argument behind it.

The format follows the pursuit.

04 · Communication

Can someone else understand it?

Students explain their work to someone who knows little about the topic. Vague thinking gets exposed in that conversation, and so does the moment a student realises they actually understand what they made.

A good C-Level ends with a student who can say

  • This was my question.
  • This is what I found.
  • This is what changed my mind.
  • This is what I made.
  • This is what I still want to know.

AI USE, MADE VISIBLE

AI is part of the pursuit.
It does not get to do the pursuing.

Students use AI to explore, compare, challenge, and organise their thinking. Answers from a model are treated as material to inspect, rather than conclusions to repeat.

Across a C-Level, students practise:

  • asking stronger follow-up questions
  • comparing AI answers with other sources
  • spotting assumptions
  • checking what might be missing
  • separating evidence from confidence
  • explaining the final work in their own words

AI use is not hidden in C-Levels. What we make visible is the thinking around it — the questions asked, the sources checked, the parts the student worked out for themselves.

THE C-LEVEL BOARD

Proof they followed the question.

Every student completes a C-Level Board: a large-format visual record of their learning pursuit.

It captures:

  • the question they pursued
  • why it mattered to them
  • what sources they used
  • how AI helped and where it fell short
  • what they found
  • what they built
  • how their thinking changed
  • what they still want to investigate

A C-Level Board sits closer to evidence than to a certificate — a record of a student thinking in public.

THE BIGGER POINT

AI changes the answer.
School still shapes the asker.

Students are growing up in a world where answers arrive before they have finished forming the question. C-Levels gives schools a way to respond to that without slowing the technology down, and without leaving students passive around it.

A C-Level works on the habits that matter before, during, and after AI use: curiosity, judgement, evidence, creation, and explanation.

That is the work an AI-exposed school still has to do.
